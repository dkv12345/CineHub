# Reference Analysis — Everyman, Alamo Drafthouse, HOYTS

These are design references, not templates. Inspect the live websites before making detailed decisions because content and interfaces can change.

## 1. Everyman Cinema

Website: https://www.everymancinema.com/

### What to study

- Premium brand positioning and editorial restraint.
- The relationship between film listings, events, coming-soon content, and venue discovery.
- How the brand makes cinema feel like a curated hospitality experience.
- Typography, spacing, image selection, and content hierarchy.

### What to borrow

- Calm confidence.
- Curated rather than noisy content presentation.
- Editorial use of imagery and whitespace.
- Brand personality that feels more considered than a generic ticket marketplace.

### What not to copy

- Exact page structure, logos, proprietary typography, colors, or imagery.
- Brand-specific hospitality features that do not exist in the target product.

## 2. Alamo Drafthouse

Website: https://drafthouse.com/

### What to study

- Clear distinctions such as Now Playing, Coming Soon, and special programming.
- Discovery filters for film type and location.
- Direct ticket actions near relevant movie content.
- A confident identity and content voice.

### What to borrow

- Strong film-programming hierarchy.
- Useful filters that help users narrow the selection.
- Clear location context.
- Calls-to-action connected to the movie/session the user is viewing.

### What not to copy

- Exact logo, brand palette, typography, promotional language, or proprietary assets.
- A dense filter system if the target product has too few movies or locations to justify it.

## 3. HOYTS

Website: https://www.hoyts.com.au/

### What to study

- Commercial movie discovery at scale.
- How movie listings, experiences, advance screenings, events, and offers are organized.
- The visibility of cinema selection and showtimes.
- How the purchase flow makes availability easier to understand.

### What to borrow

- Session-time discoverability.
- Clear separation between films, experiences, and offers.
- A purchase journey designed around real seat/session availability.
- Strong commercial information hierarchy without unnecessary decoration.

### What not to copy

- Exact interface, colors, brand assets, or content.
- Commercial categories that do not exist in the target product.

## Recommended synthesis

| Design concern          | Primary reference lens | Apply it as                                                         |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Brand atmosphere        | Everyman               | Editorial restraint and premium confidence                          |
| Film discovery          | Alamo Drafthouse       | Clear programming categories and useful filters                     |
| Booking clarity         | HOYTS                  | Session visibility, availability awareness, efficient purchase flow |
| Overall visual identity | Original target brand  | A distinct charcoal-based Cinematic Luxury system                   |

The Village Cinemas case study is useful for the principle of using darker immersive surfaces for experiential content and lighter/clearer transactional surfaces where that improves readability. Apply the principle selectively; do not assume the target project needs a light checkout if its own design system can keep dark surfaces accessible.
